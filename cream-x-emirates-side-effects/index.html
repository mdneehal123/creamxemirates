Cream X Emirates Side Effects: What Is Normal and When to Stop

Updated 2026-11-06 · General cosmetic guidance, not medical advice.

Most people have no problem or only mild dryness or tingling in the first days, like with any new face cream. Stop and see a doctor if you get burning, swelling, an itchy rash or new dark patches. Always patch test first.

Mild reactions that can happen with any new cream

  • Slight tingling or warmth for a few minutes
  • Mild dryness or tightness
  • A few small pimples in the first week on oily skin

These usually settle within 1 to 2 weeks. Use it every other night and add a plain moisturiser if needed.

Stop using it if you notice

  • Burning that does not fade, redness or swelling
  • An itchy rash, hives or small blisters
  • New dark or light patches
  • Swelling of the lips or eyes, or trouble breathing: get medical help at once

Who should not use it

The brand does not recommend it for children under 14, pregnant women or elderly people. Ask a doctor first if you are breastfeeding, have eczema, rosacea or very sensitive skin, or use prescription skin creams.

A note on any "whitening" cream

Some unbranded fairness creams sold in India and the Gulf have been found to contain steroids or mercury, which can harm the skin with long use. Buy only sealed packs with a full ingredient list, batch number and manufacturer details printed on them. See how to check an original pack.

How to patch test

  1. Apply a small amount behind the ear or on the jawline.
  2. Leave it for 24 hours.
  3. No redness, itching or swelling? You can start using it on the face.

Questions

Is Cream X Emirates safe?

Most adults can use a face cream like this without problems. Patch test first, follow the directions, and stop if you get burning, swelling or a rash.

Can it cause pimples?

A few small pimples can appear in the first week on oily skin. If they keep increasing after 2 weeks, stop and see a dermatologist.

Can pregnant women use it?

No. The brand does not recommend it during pregnancy.
